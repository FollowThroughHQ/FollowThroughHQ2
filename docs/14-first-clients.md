# Getting the first clients

The plan for the first 10 paying customers, written on 2026-10-29. Goal: **100 free playbooks sent and 10 paying customers by 31 December 2026**. A paying customer is anyone who pays anything, the $25 badge included. Budget: almost nothing; money that comes in goes back into the business. Time: 2–3 hours a day. No phone calls and no social media.

The offers are described in `01-business-plan.md`, `09-value-propositions.md`, `10-visibility-autopilot.md` and `11-verified-badge.md`. Leads are tracked in `sheets/lead-tracker.xlsx` (how to set it up is at the end of this page).

## The plan in one paragraph

Email each business yourself, one at a time. Each email names one real thing you noticed about that business, and offers something free. Half the leads are offered a **free 5-point check** of their Google profile and website. The other half get the **free playbook** for their trade. Whichever gets more replies wins. Everyone who says yes gets the free thing within a day. With the check you make **one** paid offer that fits what you found: the **$25 badge** if they'd pass it, **Visibility Autopilot** (first 30 days free) if they have a lot to fix. On top of the 100 leads, find 5 new ones a day on Google Maps. From week 3, spend a little time on partners who meet new businesses before you do.

## Which offer to lead with, and why

**Lead with the free 5-point check. Make the badge the first thing they pay for, and Autopilot the bigger offer.**

- **Your list has their websites**, so in five minutes you can find something real to mention. An email that names a real problem on *their* site shows you actually looked. Generic "I can get you more customers" emails get deleted.
- **New businesses need calls before they need sales scripts.** Your business plan says this too. The check is about getting found, which is what they're worried about right now.
- **The check is a free sample of Visibility Autopilot.** It's exactly what Starter does every month, so moving from the check to the paid service is a short step.
- **The badge is the easiest first purchase.** $25 is little enough to pay without thinking twice, and they pay only after passing. It suits new businesses with no reviews yet. It counts toward the goal, and once someone has paid you once, they trust you more.

Be honest with yourself about three things:

- **The free playbook alone won't make money.** It's useful and it gets replies, but it's about winning quotes. A business a few months old often doesn't have quotes to lose yet. That's why it's the test option, not the main one. If version B gets more replies, switch to it: the replies decide, not this page.
- **100 leads aren't enough for 10 customers.** Out of 100 cold emails, expect about 5 to 15 interested replies. Out of those, perhaps one in three buys. To get to 10 customers you need to reach about 250 businesses by mid-December, so you need the extra leads from Google Maps (below).
- **"100 playbooks sent" means almost every lead saying yes.** That won't happen with this list. Count playbooks sent from the website's signup form and from the outreach together, and treat 100 as a stretch.

### An offer worth adding (your decision)

**Google profile setup, one-time, about $79.** For businesses with no Google profile, or one that isn't verified: you set it up and fill it in, and walk them through Google's verification step. Many new businesses will fail check 1 of the badge for exactly this reason. They're the easiest people to help, and a one-off price is an easier yes than a monthly bill. If you want it, it needs a Stripe link and a line in the service terms before you sell it.

## How to reach people

### 1. Personal cold email (most of your time)

You send each email yourself, one at a time, from a normal mailbox. No mass-mailing tool: at 8–10 a day you don't need one, and emails sent by hand reach the inbox more often.

- **Why it fits you:** it works from Europe, it needs no calls, and you write well. It's the most direct way to use the 100 leads you already have.
- **When to send:** when it's 8–10 in the morning in New York: 15:00–17:00 on Eastern European time, 14:00–16:00 on Central European time. For leads further west, write the email whenever you like and use Gmail's **Schedule send** (the arrow next to **Send**) to send it at 8–9 in their morning.
- **Rules:** your real business name and address in every email. A simple way to opt out ("reply 'no thanks'"). When someone opts out, mark them "Do not contact" and never email them again. No more than 2 follow-ups. No texts (US texting rules are strict).

### 2. Partners (a little time, from week 3)

Some people meet new service businesses before you do: **insurance agents** who sell contractor insurance (every new business has to buy it), **bookkeepers and accountants** who work with tradespeople, and the free advisers at **Small Business Development Centers (SBDCs) and SCORE**. Offer them your free guides and playbooks to hand to their new clients. One good partner can send more businesses than a month of cold email. It's slow to start, though, so it won't bring most of the first 10. Spend about 2–3 emails a day on it from week 3. Don't offer referral fees yet: some insurance agents aren't allowed to take them.

### Which mailbox to send from (your decision)

Recommended: **a second web address just for outreach** (for example `getfollowthrough.com`, about $12 a year), with a mailbox on it in your pen name. If a few people mark your emails as spam, only that address suffers. Your main address keeps emailing playbooks to people who sign up on the website. Zoho Mail has a free plan for a mailbox like this (check it still does). Google Workspace costs about $7 a month. A new mailbox needs about two weeks of normal use before it sends 10 cold emails a day. While you're checking leads in week 1, send a few normal emails from it, then start at 5 cold emails a day and go up to 10. Claude can walk you through the settings that keep your emails out of spam folders.

## Where to find more leads

Your best source is free and fast: **Google Maps**.

1. Search Google Maps for "[trade] near [city]", for example "pressure washing Tampa FL". Start with the 15 trades that have a playbook, in mid-sized cities.
2. Look for businesses with **0–10 reviews**. That usually means they're new, and they're exactly the people the check and the badge are for.
3. Open their website and find the owner's first name and email (the About or Contact page). If there's only a contact form, skip them for now.
4. Add them to the sheet with source "Google Maps". About 4 minutes each, so 5 a day takes 20 minutes and gives about 100 a month.

Other free sources, slower but full of new businesses:

- **State business registers.** Every state has a free business search on its Secretary of State website, and some states let you download lists of new registrations (Florida's Sunbiz does, for example). You get the name and the date it opened, then find the website and email yourself.
- **New contractor licences.** Several states publish lists of newly licensed contractors (Texas does for HVAC and electrical, for example). Good for HVAC, plumbing and electrical.
- **City open-data websites.** Many big cities publish new business licences with the start date (Chicago, Seattle and Austin, for example).
- **Website signups.** Anyone who asks for a playbook on the site is a warm lead. Add them with source "Website signup" and send them the check offer a few days later.

## Checking a lead

For each lead, open their website on your phone and search their business name on Google Maps, then fill in the row:

- **When it opened, roughly.** The most reliable source is the state business register (search "[state] business entity search"), which shows the date the business was registered. Other clues: the date of their oldest Google review, the year in their website's footer, or when their web address was registered (look it up at who.is).
- **Google profile.** Verified, not verified (it shows "Own this business?" or "Claim this business") or none found. Note how many reviews it has.
- **One first-line finding,** the most useful real thing you noticed. Common ones:
  - On a phone, the phone number at the top can't be tapped to call.
  - No Google profile, or one that isn't verified.
  - The Google profile has no hours, no photos or no list of services.
  - The website takes more than 5 seconds to load on a phone (check at pagespeed.web.dev).
  - The phone number or business name differs between the website and Google.
  - No reviews on the website, or a quote form that doesn't work.
- If you find nothing wrong, write "looks good" and send them version B (the playbook).
- If their trade has no playbook, send them version A. The trades with a playbook: appliance repair, car detailing, drywall, electrical, handyman, hauling and transportation, house cleaning, HVAC, junk removal, landscaping, lawn care, painting, plumbing, pressure washing, roofing.

## Answers to the objections you'll hear

- **"Who are you? Is this a scam?"** "Fair question. I'm [Your name]. FollowThrough is a small business, and I work with US service businesses by email from Europe. The check is free, and the badge is paid only after you pass, so you never pay for something you haven't seen. The website is followthroughguides.com."
- **"How did you get my email?"** Say honestly where it came from (their website, or a public business list). Then: "If you'd rather not hear from me, I'll take you off right away." If they ask, take them off and mark them "Do not contact".
- **"I'm busy / I have enough work."** "Good to hear. The check takes you two minutes to read, and it's there for when things slow down."
- **"Someone already does my website."** "Great, forward them the check. Most of the fixes take them minutes."
- **"How much does it cost?"** "The check is free. If you want more: the badge is $25 one time, and the monthly service starts at $99, first 30 days free, cancel any time by email."
- **"I can't spend money right now."** "That's why everything in the check is something you can fix yourself for free. Come back when it's busier."
- **"Why do you need access to my Google profile?"** "As a manager, not the owner. You keep full control and can remove me in two clicks at any time. I need it to post, reply to reviews and pull your numbers for the report."
- **"Does the badge actually get me more work?"** "I can't promise that. What it does is give a first-time visitor, who's never heard of you and sees few reviews, an independent reason to trust you. You also get a profile and a link in our directory."
- **"You're in Europe?"** "Yes. Everything works by email, which fits a day on the job: you read it when you have a minute, and I reply while you sleep."

## What to count, and when to change course

The sheet counts these by itself: new emails sent, replies, interested replies, playbooks sent, checks sent, offers made, new customers and money in. It also shows which first email (A or B) gets more interested replies.

| By | Aim for |
|---|---|
| 20 November | The first 100 leads emailed. 8+ interested replies. 5+ checks or playbooks sent |
| 4 December | 160+ businesses contacted. Payments working. The first 3 customers |
| 31 December | About 250 contacted. 10 paying customers |

When to change something:

- **After the first 40 emails (20 A, 20 B):** keep the version with more interested replies and send only that one.
- **Fewer than 3 interested replies out of 40:** change the subject and first lines, not the offer. Fewer than 5 out of 80: change the offer.
- **More than 3 out of 100 emails come back undelivered:** stop and check the email addresses before sending more.
- **Several people say yes to the check but nobody buys:** look at which offer you made, and ask the next one what would make it worth paying for.
- **No payment link working by 1 December:** keep the interested people warm (send them the check and the playbook) and tell them payments open "in a couple of weeks". The goal moves into January.

## The lead tracker

1. Go to drive.google.com and sign in.
2. Click **New** → **File upload** and choose `lead-tracker.xlsx` from the project's `docs/sheets` folder.
3. Right-click the uploaded file → **Open with** → **Google Sheets**, then **File** → **Save as Google Sheets**. Use that copy from now on and delete the uploaded `.xlsx`.
4. Copy the 100 leads from your list and paste them into the "Leads" tab. Match the columns: business, first name, last name, email, trade, website. Set "Source" to "First list" and "Status" to "To check".
