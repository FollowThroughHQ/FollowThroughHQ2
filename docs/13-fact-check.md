# 13 · Fact check of the guides (2026-10-29)

All 15 guides were checked before going live: the licensing and legal rules against state and federal sources, the insurance ranges against published averages, and the sums in every worked pricing example. This replaces the "not confirmed" lists in PR #1, which covered 9 of the guides.

## Fixed

- **Electrical, licensing:** Pennsylvania no longer belongs with the states that leave licensing to cities. Its governor signed a statewide electrician license (SB 1260) on 2026-10-22. It phases in over the next few years, so local licenses still apply for now. New York is still local only.
- **Electrical, insurance:** general liability was $1,500–$5,000 a year. Published averages for electricians are about $700–$1,000 (Insureon $684–$732, Insurance.com $960), so it's now $600–$2,500.
- **Hauling & Transportation, interstate rules:** the guide said a USDOT number, MC authority, BOC-3 and UCR were needed only for vehicles rated 10,001 lb or more. In fact FMCSA operating authority, a USDOT number and BOC-3 are needed for for-hire interstate hauling whatever the vehicle size (a few exceptions: exempt goods, commercial zones). The 10,001 lb line applies to UCR and the $750,000 liability minimum.
- **Painting and Drywall, Oregon:** "a license for any paid work" is now "nearly any". Oregon exempts jobs under $1,000 that need no permit, but only for people who don't advertise as contractors (ORS 701.010, OAR 812-002-0120).

## Confirmed

- **Roofing:** state roofing licenses in Florida (DBPR), Arizona (ROC) and California; no state license in Texas. Insurance range fits Insureon's $3,200–$3,800 average.
- **HVAC:** Texas 48 of 72 months; California C-20 four years and $25,000 bond (all California contractor bonds since 2023); EPA 608 rules; R-410A May 2026 rule; the FTC cooling-off rule covers replacements sold during a repair visit.
- **Plumbing, Painting, Handyman, Landscaping, Junk Removal:** the rules PR #1 already linked to official sources weren't researched again. Checked this time: California's $1,000 minor-work limit needs no permit and no hired help, and ads must say "not licensed". Arizona's $1,000 handyman limit is unchanged: bills to raise it to $2,500 (2023) and $3,500 (2025) died.
- **House Cleaning:** Texas taxes cleaning sold by a company, with the household-employee exception (Publication 94-111); Florida taxes only nonresidential cleaning.
- **Car Detailing:** Washington taxes detailing, now confirmed on the Department of Revenue's own list of taxable services.
- **Electrical:** Texas requires the business to hold or employ a master electrician; California requires certified electricians for C-10 contractors (Labor Code 108.2).
- **Appliance Repair:** California service dealer registration with BHGS, fees per location, written estimate before repair; EPA Type I covers small appliances.
- **Lawn Care:** Florida's separate commercial fertilizer certification (since 2014); Texas pesticide license for hire, none for fertilizer only.
- **Pricing examples:** the sums in all 15 worked examples add up.
- **Insurance:** the other trades' general liability ranges fit published averages (appliance repair about $800, drywall about $690, pressure washing about $900, lawn care about $550 before pesticide cover).

## Still estimates (can't be confirmed from one source)

- Tool, equipment, vehicle, software and working-capital ranges in every cost table. They look reasonable but come from general knowledge; real quotes beat them.
- The inputs to the worked examples (labor rates, hours, material prices). The guides already say "treat this as an example".
- Busy and slow seasons, payment terms (net-30) and similar trade observations.

## Check again later

- **R-410A:** the May 2026 rule is challenged in court (ALTA Refrigeration v. EPA, D.C. Circuit No. 26-1202, filed 2026-07-24). If it's overturned, the HVAC guide's install rule changes.
- **Pennsylvania electricians:** once the state board is set up, add the date the statewide license becomes required.
- **New York cleaning tax:** sources disagree on whether regular contracts of 30 days or more are exempt. The guide stays vague ("taxes some interior cleaning") on purpose.
