# The Roofing Playbook

**What to say from the first call to the five-star review.** Copy the lines, put them in your own words, and keep this on your phone.

## 1. The first call or message

Sound calm, understand the job, and book a next step. Ask:

- What's going on: a leak, storm damage, missing shingles or an old roof?
- Is water coming in right now? If so, offer to tarp or patch it first.
- How old is the roof, and has it been repaired before?
- Is there an insurance claim open for it?

> “Thanks for calling [Business], this is [Name]. Tell me what's going on with the roof and I'll see how we can help.”

> “Can you text me a photo from the ground? I can come out Tuesday at 10 or Wednesday at 2. Which works better?”

## 2. Presenting the quote

- Go through it in person or by phone, not as a number in an email.
- Start with your photos from the roof: show them what you found before you talk price.
- Itemize tear-off, underlayment, shingles, flashing, cleanup and warranty, and price rotten decking per sheet.
- Offer two or three shingle options when it fits, and say how long the quote holds.

> “Here are the photos from your roof. Here's how I'd fix it, and here's the price. I can start the week of the [date]. What questions do you have?”

## 3. The 7 objections roofers hear and what to say

Acknowledge it, ask a question, answer honestly, suggest a next step.

**1. “Your price is higher than the other guy's.”**
“That's fair, I'd compare too. What's included in the other quote? Materials, underlayment, warranty and cleanup are where prices differ. I'd rather you choose on what you get than on the number alone.”

**2. “I need to get a few more quotes.”**
“Totally reasonable. To make it easy, compare the shingle brand, underlayment, flashing, warranty and who does the work. I'll check back Thursday to answer any questions.”

**3. “Can you do it cheaper?”**
“I can adjust the materials or scope to hit a budget. What number are you hoping for? Then I'll show you what that gets. I won't cut corners on the parts that keep your roof dry.”

**4. “My insurance should pay for it.”**
“If it's storm damage, I can document the roof with photos for your adjuster. I can't waive your deductible, because that's against the rules in many states, but I'll make sure the damage is well documented.”

**5. “I'll wait until next year.”**
“Understood. Small leaks can turn into rotted decking, which costs a lot more. Want me to patch the leak now so you have time to plan the replacement?”

**6. “How do I know you'll be around for the warranty?”**
“Fair question. Here's my license and insurance, and local references you can call. I put the warranty in writing so you're covered either way.”

**7. “Why do you need a deposit?”**
“The deposit pays for your materials so they're ordered on time. It's all in the contract, and the balance is due once the work is finished and you've approved it.”

## 4. Follow up: day 2, day 5, day 12

Most quotes go quiet, not “no”. Keep each message short and useful.

> **Day 2:** “Hi [Name], it's [Your name] from [Business]. Did the roof quote come through okay? Happy to go through the photos with you again.”

> **Day 5:** “Hi [Name], checking in on your roof. I have an opening the week of the [date] and can hold it for you until Friday.”

> **Day 12:** “Hi [Name], I'll close out your quote on Friday so I can plan the schedule. If you'd still like to go ahead, or just fix the leak for now, reply and I'll hold a spot.”

After strong wind or hail in their area, one more message is welcome: “Hi [Name], we had a big storm Tuesday. If you spot shingles in the yard, send me a photo and I'll tell you if it's urgent.”

## 5. Ask for the review and the referral

Ask right after the job, when they're happiest, and text a direct link to your Google review page.

> “Thanks again, [Name]. If you're happy with the roof, would you leave a quick Google review? I'll text you the link. And if a neighbor mentions their roof, I'd be glad to take a look.”

Neighbors notice a roof going on. With the owner's okay, leave a yard sign up for a week, and introduce yourself on the street where door-to-door is allowed.

## Three rules that keep you out of trouble

- Never offer to waive or cover a deductible. It's illegal in many states.
- Check your state and city rules on licensing, permits and door-to-door sales before you chase storm work.
- Put scope, price, payment schedule and warranty in writing, with a per-sheet price for hidden rotten decking.
