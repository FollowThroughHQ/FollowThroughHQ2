# The Pressure Washing Playbook

**What to say from the first call to the five-star review.** Copy the lines, put them in your own words, and keep this on your phone.

## 1. The first call or message

Sound calm, understand the job, and book a next step. Ask:

- What needs cleaning: the house, the driveway, a deck, a patio or a fence?
- What's the siding: vinyl, brick, stucco or painted wood? One story or two?
- Any oil or rust stains, or green growth on the shady side?
- Is there an outdoor water tap I can use?

> “Thanks for calling [Business], this is [Name]. Tell me what needs cleaning and I'll see how we can help.”

> “Can you text me photos and the address? I can quote today and come Tuesday or Friday. Which works better?”

## 2. Presenting the quote

- Price flat surfaces by the square foot and house washes by the size of the home, with a minimum per job.
- Say how you'll clean each surface: soft wash for siding, a surface cleaner for concrete.
- Write down what won't come out, like old oil stains or rust, before you start.
- Offer add-ons that cost little extra once you're there: walkways, steps, trash bins.

> “Here's what I'll clean, how I'll clean it and the price. I'll rinse your plants before and after. I can do it on [day]. What questions do you have?”

## 3. The 7 objections pressure washers hear and what to say

Acknowledge it, ask a question, answer honestly, suggest a next step.

**1. “Another company quoted less.”**
“That's fair, compare away. Are they soft washing your siding or blasting it with high pressure? Ask about insurance and whether they rinse your plants. I'll send before and after photos of a house like yours so you can see the result.”

**2. “I need to run it by the property manager.”**
“Of course. Who should I send it to? I'll send a one-page summary with the scope, my certificate of insurance and how I handle wash water, so they can approve it quickly.”

**3. “Can you do it cheaper?”**
“Let's see. Could we do the driveway and patio now and the house later? Booking with a neighbor on the same day also lowers the price. I won't skip rinsing your plants or blocking drains, because that's what protects your property.”

**4. “I'll just rent a pressure washer.”**
“Plenty of people do. Have you used one on siding or a wood deck? High pressure can strip paint, splinter wood and push water behind siding. Many customers do the patio themselves and hire us for the house. I can quote just that.”

**5. “It can wait until next year.”**
“That can work. Is there green or black growth on the shady side? Algae and mold keep spreading, make walkways slippery and get harder to remove the longer they sit. I can do the worst areas now and book you early for spring.”

**6. “Will it damage my house or kill my plants?”**
“Fair question, it happens with careless cleaners. Do you have delicate plants or older paint? I use low pressure on siding, rinse your plants before and after, and do a test spot first. I'm insured for damage, and here's my certificate.”

**7. “Why do I have to pay the minimum for a small job?”**
“Fair question. What else needs cleaning while I'm there? Every job takes setup, travel and cleanup whatever the size, so the minimum covers that. Adding a walkway, steps or trash bins usually costs little extra and makes the visit worth it.”

## 4. Follow up: day 2, day 5, day 12

Most quotes go quiet, not “no”. Keep each message short and useful.

> **Day 2:** “Hi [Name], it's [Your name] from [Business]. Did the quote come through okay? Happy to answer any questions.”

> **Day 5:** “Hi [Name], I'm working on your street on [day]. If you'd like to go ahead, I can do yours the same day.”

> **Day 12:** “Hi [Name], I'll close out your quote on Friday. If you'd like just the driveway for now, reply and I'll fit you in.”

Homeowners often book within days of seeing a neighbor's clean driveway. When you finish a job, quote the houses on either side.

## 5. Ask for the review and the referral

Ask right after the job, when the clean surface is still wet and shining, and text a direct link to your Google review page.

> “Thanks again, [Name]. If you're happy with how it looks, would you leave a quick Google review with a before-and-after photo? I'll text you the link. And if a neighbor wants theirs done, I'd be glad to help.”

Offer a yearly reminder. Algae comes back, and a customer who books every spring is worth far more than one wash.

## Three rules that keep you out of trouble

- Keep wash water out of storm drains, especially on commercial jobs. One complaint can bring a big fine.
- Confirm your liability insurance covers damage to the property you clean: etched concrete, stripped paint, dead plants.
- Walk around before you start, and write down stains, loose boards and plants near the house.
