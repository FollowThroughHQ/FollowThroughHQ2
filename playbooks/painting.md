# The Painting Playbook

**What to say from the first call to the five-star review.** Copy the lines, put them in your own words, and keep this on your phone.

## 1. The first call or message

Sound calm, understand the job, and book a next step. Ask:

- Inside or outside, and which rooms or sides of the house?
- Same colors, or a change? Dark to light usually means an extra coat.
- Is there peeling, cracking or bare wood? When was the house built? (Before 1978 means lead-safe rules.)
- When do you need it done, and is there a date like a party or a sale?

> “Thanks for calling [Business], this is [Name]. Tell me about the project and I'll see how we can help.”

> “Can you send a few photos? I can come by to measure Tuesday at 10 or Wednesday at 4. Which works better?”

## 2. Presenting the quote

- List every wall, ceiling, trim piece and door, the number of coats, the paint line and the sheen.
- Put prep on its own line: scraping, patching, caulking, priming. It's what makes the job last.
- Offer good, better, best paint lines when it fits, and say who moves the furniture.
- Give a written schedule and say how long the quote holds.

> “Here's what's included, here's how we'll prep it, and here's the price. I can start on [day]. What questions do you have?”

## 3. The 7 objections painters hear and what to say

Acknowledge it, ask a question, answer honestly, suggest a next step.

**1. “Another painter quoted a lot less.”**
“That's fair, I'd compare too. How many coats, what paint and how much prep is in their quote? That's where painting prices differ. I'll send you photos of a two-year-old job of mine so you can see how the prep holds up.”

**2. “I'm getting a few more quotes.”**
“Good idea. What matters most to you, the finish or the timeline? Ask each painter the same things: paint brand and line, number of coats, what prep they do, and whether they're lead-safe certified if your home is from before 1978. I'll check in Tuesday.”

**3. “Can you knock something off?”**
“I can trim the scope to fit a budget, like one coat on walls staying the same color, or ceilings later. What number did you have in mind? I won't skip prep, because that's what keeps paint from peeling.”

**4. “We'll just paint it ourselves.”**
“Plenty of people do. Which part are you dreading most? Many customers paint the easy walls and hire out ceilings, stairwells and trim. I can quote just those parts.”

**5. “Let's wait until next spring.”**
“That can work. Have you noticed peeling or bare wood yet? Once wood is exposed, water gets in, and next year's job can include rot repair. I can spot-prime the worst areas now and book you early for spring.”

**6. “Will you finish on time, and will my house be a mess?”**
“Fair question, painters get that a lot. What's your deadline? I'll give you a written schedule, cover floors and furniture, and clean up every day. Here are three recent customers you can call.”

**7. “Why do you want a deposit?”**
“It pays for your paint and supplies, which I order in your colors. How would you like to pay? It's in the contract, and the balance is due after we walk through the job together and you're happy.”

## 4. Follow up: day 2, day 5, day 12

Most quotes go quiet, not “no”. Keep each message short and useful.

> **Day 2:** “Hi [Name], it's [Your name] from [Business]. Did the painting quote come through okay? Happy to answer any questions about colors or prep.”

> **Day 5:** “Hi [Name], here are a few photos of a similar job we finished last month. I have an opening on [day] and can hold it for you until Friday.”

> **Day 12:** “Hi [Name], I'll close out your quote on Friday so I can plan the schedule. If you'd still like to go ahead, reply and I'll hold a spot.”

People compare painters for weeks. Photos of a finished room like theirs do more than any reminder.

## 5. Ask for the review and the referral

Ask at the final walk-through, when they're happiest, and text a direct link to your Google review page.

> “Thanks again, [Name]. If you're happy with how it looks, would you leave a quick Google review with a photo? I'll text you the link. And if a friend is thinking about painting, I'd be glad to help.”

Leave a labeled can of each color for touch-ups, with your number on the lid.

## Three rules that keep you out of trouble

- On homes built before 1978, get EPA lead-safe certified and hand out the Renovate Right pamphlet before you sand or scrape.
- Carry cover for property damage and overspray. One spilled gallon can cost more than the job paid.
- Put the scope in writing, and add a clause for rotten wood found while scraping, priced per linear foot.
